CSCE 655


Human-Centered Computing

A reading- and discussion-driven seminar on frontier HCI research

This is a frontier HCI class covering human-centered computing and a range of late-breaking topics in the design and development of human-computer interaction. Each week combines three modules — lecture, student presentation, and discussion — built around three papers spanning theory and highly technical, futuristic interaction devices.

You will present twice a semester, as a group of two, on a topic of your choice, and lead the class discussion.

14 weeks 3 papers / week 2 presentations / student Lecture · Presentation · Discussion
Learning Outcomes
  • Engage in collaborative and discursive research practices within human-centered computing.
  • Build deep understanding of state-of-the-art research across access computing, physical computing, haptics, Human-AI interaction, and more.
  • Evaluate the contributions, limitations, and gaps in current research, and articulate findings through class presentations.
  • Collaborate with peers to select, study, and present technical HCI research, pitching a research proposal to the class.
  • Identify compelling research questions and justify their significance within the course themes and the broader HCI field.
  • Explore design principles, challenges, and societal implications of mobile computing, AR/VR/XR, assistive technology, and privacy/security.
  • Develop technical writing and academic communication skills, producing well-structured research proposals.
